🔍 Market Opportunity & Founder Bottlenecks

【Market Pain & Gap】In multilingual churches, part of the congregation cannot follow the sermon every service. Hiring human interpreters is expensive and hard to schedule, and small churches cannot afford it; generic translation apps do not know service terminology or flow, and their latency and accuracy fall short. The original Glossa was built by Anton K. with Claude Code and passed $20k revenue by May 2026.
【Opportunity & Arbitrage】Do not build generic translation; own one scene end to end. A church service is a fixed flow (sermon, prayer, hymns), so the terminology base and latency can be tuned for exactly that flow. Churches pay per language per hour, and 100 active subscriptions support $14,404 MRR and $196,539 all-time revenue (Stripe-verified via TrustMRR). Copycats need not fixate on churches: conferences, tour groups, international schools and court interpreting share the same 'fixed flow + multilingual audience' structure, and the same pipeline ports over with a swapped terminology base.

🛠️ 4-Step Operational Blueprint (SOP)

Phase 01 · Pick a vertical scene with a fixed flow and a multilingual audience Reverse Insight

Start from the scene, not the tech. List occasions near you with a fixed flow and a multilingual audience (church services, conference talks, tour groups, international-school parent meetings). Talk to 3-5 real institutions about how they solve translation today and what they would pay. Churches are proven, but copycats should pick a scene they can actually reach.

🏁 Deliverable:1 target scene + willingness-to-pay interviews with 3 institutions
Phase 02 · Speech-to-text + translation + caption pipeline working Rapid Build

Use off-the-shelf STT APIs for audio-to-text, a translation API, then push translated text or audio to listeners' phones over WebSocket for low latency. Start with one segment only (the sermon): build a 200-term scene glossary (common sermon words, hymn names, people's names) and target under 3 seconds of latency. Do not train your own models; call existing APIs.

🏁 Deliverable:End-to-end live translation demo + glossary + measured latency data
Phase 03 · Pay-per-hour plus monthly plans, billed through Stripe Commercialization

Copy the original pricing structure: occasional use billed per language per hour ($5/hour/language), regulars on monthly plans ($99/$299/$499 by language count and sessions). Bill subscriptions and metered usage through Stripe. Watch STT and translation API unit prices: translation costs are falling, so leave room to cut prices instead of locking in a high price.

🏁 Deliverable:Stripe billing loop + pricing page + per-session cost sheet
Phase 04 · Knock on institutions' doors one by one, offer one free trial service Cold Outbound

There is no paid-ads shortcut here: contact target institutions directly, offer one free trial service with you on site as backup, and let the congregation scan a code to hear the translation live. Win 3-5 seed institutions, then expand through denominational networks and peer referrals. In parallel, swap the glossary to open a second scene (conferences or tours) so all eggs are not in one scene.

🏁 Deliverable:3-5 seed institutions + trial-to-paid conversion data + second-scene glossary

⚠️ Pitfalls & Risk Warning

Evidence freshness warning: the original's Stripe-verified figures were last synced 19 October 2026; the feed died when the API key expired, so $14,404 MRR is a 19 October snapshot, not today's number. The 30-day snapshot ($31,173) and the daily-sum ($12,903) disagree on method; use MRR as the reference. Translation API costs keep falling and will squeeze prices long-term, so do not project today's margins forward. Avoid scenes with no clear payer (pure charity, one-off events); institutional customers are the ones who renew. Live-audio latency and glossary mistranslations kill reputation, so field-test at least 3 real services before launch.

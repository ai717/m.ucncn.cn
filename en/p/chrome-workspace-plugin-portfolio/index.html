🔍 Market Opportunity & Founder Bottlenecks

The biggest trap for indie developers is not writing code but spending a year building something nobody buys: one year on a single idea, then learning the hard way that nobody wants it. Betting everything on one product means betting everything on one guess, and one miss costs a year. The fix is to turn product building into batch betting with presale validation: one feature per app, distributed through the Chrome Web Store and Google Workspace Marketplace instead of building traffic from zero; put up a buy button first with a 10-30 day delivery promise, build only when orders arrive, kill the idea when nobody buys, so each miss costs days not a year. Out of 30+ bets, 8 paid off, with MagicSlides and Sheet AI each catching their own long-tail demand. One YouTube channel per product means videos from years ago still bring orders today, so the assets appreciate with time. AI coding has pushed the cost of each bet close to zero, which is what makes 22 failures survivable.

🛠️ 4-Step Operational Blueprint (SOP)

➔ Phase 01: Collect 10 candidate ideas from TrustMRR revenue data and your own pain. Follow the right people on X, keep only ideas whose users can pay, cut student and curiosity-only ideas, and write one line per idea stating who pays and why.
➔ Phase 02: Sell before you build. Ship a landing page per idea the same day with an AI coding tool, add a buy button and a 10-30 day delivery promise, start the 10-day build clock only on the first order, kill ideas with no orders, keep one feature per app; the included portfolio_tracker.py script runs portfolio accounting, win-rate stats and kill-or-keep decisions.
➔ Phase 03: Close the commercial loop. List each product on the Chrome Web Store and Google Workspace Marketplace for built-in search and install flow, mix subscription with one-time purchase, run AppSumo lifetime deals for first sales and reviews, collect with Stripe, and track each product separately with a monthly review of where time is worth spending.
➔ Phase 04: Cold-start distribution. Open one YouTube channel per product (mixing audiences in one channel confuses everyone), post about 10 short phone-shot how-to videos, take the first sale from a matching Facebook group, then AppSumo; videos need consistency not polish, old videos keep selling for years; review MRR monthly and cut any product with three straight months of zero growth to free time for winners.

## 🎯 Module 2: Core Value Proposition & Arbitrage Wedge

### Market Friction & User Pain Points
In multilingual churches, part of the congregation cannot follow the sermon every service. Hiring human interpreters is expensive and hard to schedule, and small churches cannot afford it; generic translation apps do not know service terminology or flow, and their latency and accuracy fall short. The original Glossa was built by Anton K. with Claude Code and passed $20k revenue by May 2026.

### Opportunity & Unfair Arbitrage
Do not build generic translation; own one scene end to end. A church service is a fixed flow (sermon, prayer, hymns), so the terminology base and latency can be tuned for exactly that flow. Churches pay per language per hour, and 100 active subscriptions support $14,404 MRR and $196,539 all-time revenue (Stripe-verified via TrustMRR). Copycats need not fixate on churches: conferences, tour groups, international schools and court interpreting share the same 'fixed flow + multilingual audience' structure, and the same pipeline ports over with a swapped terminology base.

## 🚀 Module 3: 4-Phase Turnkey Execution SOP

### Phase 01 · Pick a vertical scene with a fixed flow and a multilingual audience [Reverse Insight]
- **Action:** Start from the scene, not the tech. List occasions near you with a fixed flow and a multilingual audience (church services, conference talks, tour groups, international-school parent meetings). Talk to 3-5 real institutions about how they solve translation today and what they would pay. Churches are proven, but copycats should pick a scene they can actually reach.
- **Deliverable:** 1 target scene + willingness-to-pay interviews with 3 institutions

### Phase 02 · Speech-to-text + translation + caption pipeline working [Rapid Build]
- **Action:** Use off-the-shelf STT APIs for audio-to-text, a translation API, then push translated text or audio to listeners' phones over WebSocket for low latency. Start with one segment only (the sermon): build a 200-term scene glossary (common sermon words, hymn names, people's names) and target under 3 seconds of latency. Do not train your own models; call existing APIs.
- **Deliverable:** End-to-end live translation demo + glossary + measured latency data

### Phase 03 · Pay-per-hour plus monthly plans, billed through Stripe [Commercialization]
- **Action:** Copy the original pricing structure: occasional use billed per language per hour ($5/hour/language), regulars on monthly plans ($99/$299/$499 by language count and sessions). Bill subscriptions and metered usage through Stripe. Watch STT and translation API unit prices: translation costs are falling, so leave room to cut prices instead of locking in a high price.
- **Deliverable:** Stripe billing loop + pricing page + per-session cost sheet

### Phase 04 · Knock on institutions' doors one by one, offer one free trial service [Cold Outbound]
- **Action:** There is no paid-ads shortcut here: contact target institutions directly, offer one free trial service with you on site as backup, and let the congregation scan a code to hear the translation live. Win 3-5 seed institutions, then expand through denominational networks and peer referrals. In parallel, swap the glossary to open a second scene (conferences or tours) so all eggs are not in one scene.
- **Deliverable:** 3-5 seed institutions + trial-to-paid conversion data + second-scene glossary
